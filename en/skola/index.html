Foundation and posts

  1. STAGE 1/⁠10 · SETTING OUT, POSTS

    01 / 28

    Setting out and the ground

    WHAT IT IS
    The cottage outline and the post points, marked on the plot.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Fixes where each support will stand.
    WITHOUT IT
    Posts go in “by eye” and the spans drift.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Check the corners by the diagonals: the two diagonals are equal.

  2. STAGE 1/⁠10 · SETTING OUT, POSTS

    02 / 28

    Posts — the support lines

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Square steel tube · concrete in the pit · span ≤ 2.0 m
    WHAT IT IS
    A square steel tube set in a pit filled with concrete; support lines with spans of no more than 2.0 m.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Lifts the cottage off the ground; the floor joists rest on it.
    WITHOUT IT
    With spans over 2 m, the floor sags.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Measure the spans with a tape: none over 2.0 m.

  3. STAGE 1/⁠10 · SETTING OUT, POSTS

    03 / 28

    Ventilated underfloor space

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Open underfloor · mesh
    WHAT IT IS
    Air moves under a cottage that stands on posts.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Damp does not collect under the floor.
    WITHOUT IT
    If the space is closed off completely, the lower OSB and the joists take on moisture.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The space is open or meshed — not boarded up solid.

Floor

  1. STAGE 2/⁠10 · FLOOR STRUCTURE

    04 / 28

    Lower OSB 9

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    OSB 9 mm — from below
    WHAT IT IS
    The bottom sheet of the floor build-up, fixed under the joists.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Holds the stone wool from below and separates it from the underfloor space.
    WITHOUT IT
    The wool falls out; animals and damp get in.
    CHECK ON SITE
    From below, the sheet is continuous, with no gaps.

  2. STAGE 2/⁠10 · FLOOR STRUCTURE

    05 / 28

    Floor joist 50×150, spacing 60

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Joist 50×150 · spacing 60 cm
    WHAT IT IS
    Load-bearing joists on the support lines.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Carry the whole floor load down to the posts.
    WITHOUT IT
    With wider spacing or a smaller section, the floor bounces like a spring.
    CHECK ON SITE
    60 cm between joists, section 5/⁠15.

  3. STAGE 2/⁠10 · FLOOR STRUCTURE

    06 / 28

    Stone wool in the floor — 150 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Stone wool 3 × 50 = 150 mm
    WHAT IT IS
    Three layers × 50 mm between the joists.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps the heat from escaping downwards.
    WITHOUT IT
    The floor is cold and the heating burns more; fewer layers do the same.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Wool up to the full joist depth, no gaps.

  4. STAGE 2/⁠10 · FLOOR STRUCTURE

    07 / 28

    OSB 18 — the top sheet of the floor

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    OSB 18 · underlay 3 · laminate 10
    WHAT IT IS
    The rigid top layer. The underlay and the laminate go on it at stage 10.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Gives the laminate a flat, firm base.
    WITHOUT IT
    With thin OSB, the laminate creaks and dents.
    CHECK ON SITE
    OSB 18 mm, sheets laid with staggered seams.

8 CARDS · ~5 MIN

Wall

  1. STAGE 3/⁠10 · WALL FRAME AND DIAGONALS

    08 / 28

    Frame — stud 5/⁠15, spacing 60

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Stud 5/⁠15 · spacing 60 cm
    WHAT IT IS
    Dry timber studs at 60 cm spacing.
    WHAT IT DOES
    The wall's load-bearing skeleton: every layer and the roof stand on it.
    WITHOUT IT
    With spacing over 60, the wool cannot sit tight and the wall weakens.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Measure the spacing with a tape; studs 5/⁠15.

    EXPLANATION

    The wall starts with a skeleton: dry timber studs, 5/⁠15 cm, 60 cm apart. Everything stands on these studs — the wool, the membrane, the roof. The spacing is 60 cm so that the wool fits tightly between the studs; with wider spacing it cannot sit tight, and the wall weakens too.

  2. STAGE 3/⁠10 · WALL FRAME AND DIAGONALS

    09 / 28

    Diagonal bracing

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Diagonal bracing — on every wall, on both sides
    WHAT IT IS
    A sloped member between the studs — on both sides of the wall.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps the frame from leaning sideways — the wall's stiffness comes from it.
    WITHOUT IT
    The frame leans into a parallelogram.
    CHECK ON SITE
    On every wall, the diagonal shows on both sides.

    EXPLANATION

    A rectangular frame is not stiff on its own: push it from the side and it turns into a parallelogram. A sloped member between the studs prevents exactly that — the wall locks into triangles and no longer leans. That is why we fit a diagonal on both sides of every wall: one diagonal cannot hold the whole house.

  3. STAGE 3/⁠10 · WALL FRAME AND DIAGONALS

    10 / 28

    Horizontal noggins

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Horizontal noggins — between the studs
    WHAT IT IS
    Short pieces set between the studs, staggered.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Tie the studs together and keep a long stud from bending.
    WITHOUT IT
    Each stud works on its own.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Noggins between the studs, on both sides.

    EXPLANATION

    A long stud standing alone tends to bend. Short horizontal noggins between the studs tie them to each other — so it is no longer each stud on its own, but the whole wall together. In neighbouring bays the noggins sit at different heights — staggered.

  4. STAGE 5/⁠10 · MEMBRANE AND BATTENS

    11 / 28

    Wind membrane — outside

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Wind membrane — outside, on the frame
    WHAT IT IS
    A breathable layer on the frame, under the facade.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Lets vapour out, and keeps wind and damp from getting in.
    WITHOUT IT
    The wool gets wet — with a membrane turned the wrong way, too.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Membrane under the battens, seams lapped top over bottom.

    EXPLANATION

    On the outer side of the frame sits the membrane — a thin layer, but a clever one: it lets the vapour coming from the rooms out, and keeps wind and rain damp from getting in. Its direction matters: a membrane turned the wrong way wets the wool. The seams overlap top over bottom, like roof tiles, so water cannot run in.

  5. STAGE 5/⁠10 · MEMBRANE AND BATTENS

    12 / 28

    Battens — 25 mm air gap

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Air gap 25 mm · battens · bottom edge — mesh
    WHAT IT IS
    Vertical battens on the membrane.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Make an air channel behind the facade — damp goes up and out.
    WITHOUT IT
    With no gap, or the bottom edge closed, the facade rots from behind.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Gap 25 mm, bottom edge open (mesh), top edge open.

    EXPLANATION

    Behind the facade board, 25 mm of air moves — in at the bottom, out at the top. After rain the board dries from both sides and rot does not start. The bottom edge has a mesh: insects and mice cannot get in, while the air passes freely.

  6. STAGE 7/⁠10 · FACADE

    13 / 28

    Timber facade 13 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Timber facade 13 mm — on battens
    WHAT IT IS
    The outer boards, on the battens.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Shields from the weather and gives the house its face.
    WITHOUT IT
    Nailed straight onto the membrane, the facade has no air gap.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The boards are on battens, not on the membrane.

    EXPLANATION

    The 13 mm facade board is fixed to the battens, not to the membrane — it is the batten that leaves the air gap behind it. The board shields from the weather and gives the house its face, but how long it lasts depends on what is behind it: a facade nailed straight onto the membrane cannot dry.

  7. STAGE 9/⁠10 · INSULATION AND VAPOUR BARRIER

    14 / 28

    Stone wool in the wall — 3 × 50 = 150 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Stone wool 3 × 50 = 150 mm · 50 kg/⁠m³
    WHAT IT IS
    Three layers between the studs, 50 kg/⁠m³.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps the warmth inside.
    WITHOUT IT
    With one layer, or thin wool, the wall is cold.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Three layers, no gaps, to the full stud depth.

    EXPLANATION

    Between the studs is stone wool — three 50 mm layers, 150 mm in all, density 50 kg/⁠m³. It is this layer that keeps the warmth in. Not only the thickness matters, but how it lies: to the full stud depth, with no gaps — every gap is a cold bridge. One layer, or a thin one, and the wall is cold.

  8. STAGE 9/⁠10 · INSULATION AND VAPOUR BARRIER

    15 / 28

    Vapour membrane — inside

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Vapour membrane — inside, on the warm side
    WHAT IT IS
    A barrier over the wool, on the warm side.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps room vapour out of the wall, so the wool stays dry.
    WITHOUT IT
    In winter, condensation forms in the wool.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Before the interior panel goes on: seams taped.

    EXPLANATION

    Room air is warm and humid — breathing, cooking, the shower. That vapour pushes towards the wall. The vapour membrane lies over the wool on the warm side and keeps the vapour out of the wall, so the wool stays dry. Without it, condensation forms in the wool in winter. We tape the seams: with an open seam, the barrier is no longer a barrier.

Roof

  1. STAGE 4/⁠10 · ROOF FRAME

    16 / 28

    Roof frame — rafters

    WHAT IT IS
    The roof's load-bearing members, from the wall to the ridge, evenly spaced.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Carry the whole roof load — snow, wind — down to the walls.
    WITHOUT IT
    With too wide a spacing, the metal sheet sags.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Even spacing, the same section throughout.

  2. STAGE 5/⁠10 · MEMBRANE AND BATTENS

    17 / 28

    Wind membrane on the roof

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Wind membrane — under the sheet, below the battens
    WHAT IT IS
    A breathable membrane on the rafters, under the metal sheet.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps condensation from the underside of the sheet off the wool.
    WITHOUT IT
    The sheet “sweats” onto the wool.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Membrane under the battens, run down into the gutter.

  3. STAGE 5/⁠10 · MEMBRANE AND BATTENS

    18 / 28

    Roof battens — ~30 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Battens ~30 mm — on the membrane
    WHAT IT IS
    Battens on the membrane, with a ventilation gap.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Give the sheet support and air.
    WITHOUT IT
    The sheet lies straight on the membrane, with no gap.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The batten spacing matches the sheet profile.

  4. STAGE 6/⁠10 · ROOF COVERING AND DRAINAGE

    19 / 28

    Coloured metal sheet 0.5 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Coloured metal sheet 0.5 mm · screws with rubber washers
    WHAT IT IS
    The outer cover, fixed with screws on washers.
    WHAT IT DOES
    The first shield: it sends the water to the gutter.
    WITHOUT IT
    Too thin, it flutters in the wind and dents at the screws.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Thickness 0.5 on the invoice; screws with rubber washers.

  5. STAGE 6/⁠10 · ROOF COVERING AND DRAINAGE

    20 / 28

    Drainage — gutter and downpipe

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Gutter + downpipe
    WHAT IT IS
    A gutter along the roof edge, a downpipe below.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Leads the water off through the gutter, so it does not sit on the structure or the facade.
    WITHOUT IT
    Water runs down the facade and washes out the foot of the posts.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The gutter falls towards the pipe; the pipe takes the water away from the facade.

  6. STAGE 9/⁠10 · INSULATION AND VAPOUR BARRIER

    21 / 28

    Ceiling — stone wool 150 + vapour membrane + panel 13

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Stone wool 150 · vapour membrane · interior panel 13
    WHAT IT IS
    The same layers as in the wall.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps the heat from escaping upwards.
    WITHOUT IT
    With less wool in the ceiling, much of the house's heat leaves through the roof.
    CHECK ON SITE
    150 mm in the ceiling too; the barrier on the warm side.

    WHAT IT IS
    PVC with double glazing as standard; aluminium as an option.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Lets the light in and keeps the warmth from getting out.
    WITHOUT IT

    CHECK ON SITE

  2. STAGE 8/⁠10 · OPENINGS

    23 / 28

    Door

    WHAT IT IS
    The entrance door in the opening frame.
    WHAT IT DOES
    The first barrier against weather and intruders.
    WITHOUT IT
    With an opening out of square, the door soon stops closing.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The door closes freely; the opening's diagonals are equal.

  3. STAGE 8/⁠10 · OPENINGS

    24 / 28

    Sealing the opening

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Tape + foam · membranes folded into the opening
    WHAT IT IS
    Tape and foam between the window frame and the wall frame, membranes folded into the opening.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Keeps wind and water from getting in around the frame.
    WITHOUT IT
    The window is warm, but around the frame it is cold and wet.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The foam is covered with tape, not left open.

    25 / 28

    Interior timber panel 13 mm

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Interior timber panel 13 mm
    WHAT IT IS
    A finished panel over the vapour membrane.
    WHAT IT DOES
    The inner face of the wall; it protects the barrier.
    WITHOUT IT
    Panelled straight over the wool, the wall has no barrier.
    CHECK ON SITE
    At the edge, the membrane shows under the panel.

  2. STAGE 10/⁠10 · INTERIOR PANEL, FLOOR, UTILITY ZONE, TERRACE

    26 / 28

    Facade oil

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Oil renewed — once every 4 years
    WHAT IT IS
    Clear (as standard) or tinted oil on the timber boards.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Protects the wood from UV light and damp.
    WITHOUT IT
    Not renewed, the wood greys and cracks.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Renewal once every 4 years — put it in the calendar.

  3. STAGE 10/⁠10 · INTERIOR PANEL, FLOOR, UTILITY ZONE, TERRACE

    27 / 28

    Terrace — decking, railing, steps

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Decking on posts · with gaps · clear of the ground
    WHAT IT IS
    Open or covered; priced separately.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Adds outdoor space to the cottage.
    WITHOUT IT
    If the decking touches the ground, it rots from below.
    CHECK ON SITE
    Decking on posts, with gaps, clear of the ground.

  4. STAGE 10/⁠10 · INTERIOR PANEL, FLOOR, UTILITY ZONE, TERRACE

    28 / 28

    Utility zone — outside

    THIS IS HOW WE BUILD
    Boiler and electrical cabinet — outside, on the back wall
    WHAT IT IS
    The boiler and the electrical cabinet, in an annex or a cabinet on the back wall.
    WHAT IT DOES
    Takes no room inside — noise and damp stay outside.
    WITHOUT IT
    Built into the bathroom, it brings damp and servicing into the house.
    CHECK ON SITE
    The annex has its own door and ventilation.
